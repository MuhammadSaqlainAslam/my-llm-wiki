## TL;DR

IRIS replaces the recurrent (LSTM-based) world model used by Dreamer-style agents with a discrete autoencoder plus an autoregressive [[Transformer]], treating world modeling as a sequence-modeling problem over a vocabulary of image tokens rather than continuous latent vectors. With the equivalent of only two hours of Atari gameplay, IRIS reaches a mean human-normalized score of 1.046 on Atari 100k, outperforming humans on 10 of 26 games.

---

## The Idea

A discrete autoencoder tokenizes each observed frame into a sequence of discrete codes, similar in spirit to how a VQ-VAE tokenizes images. An autoregressive Transformer then learns to predict the next frame's tokens given past tokens and actions — the same next-token-prediction objective used in language modeling, just applied to a vocabulary of image tokens instead of words. A policy is then trained entirely on imagined rollouts generated by this Transformer-based world model.

---

## Why It Matters

- One of the first demonstrations that a Transformer-based world model, treating dynamics prediction as sequence modeling, could outperform recurrent (LSTM/RSSM-based) world models like Dreamer on sample efficiency
- Reframes world modeling explicitly through the lens of language-model-style next-token prediction, foreshadowing later token-based approaches (Genie's action tokens, GameNGen's frame-by-frame generation)
- Successor work (e.g. "Learning to Play Atari in a World of Tokens") explicitly frames IRIS as relying on CNNs/LSTMs for the policy stage, not the world model itself — a nuance worth preserving rather than overstating IRIS's architecture as fully Transformer end-to-end

---

## Limitations

- Policy learning (as opposed to the world model itself) in the original IRIS still uses CNN/LSTM components, which later work argues may limit performance relative to a fully discrete-representation approach
- Evaluated primarily on Atari 100k — a well-established benchmark, but narrower than DreamerV3's 150+ task evaluation
