# Step 3.5 Flash: Open Frontier-Level Intelligence with 11B Active Parameters

## TL;DR

Step 3.5 Flash is a sparse [[Mixture-of-Experts]] model built specifically for agents: sharp reasoning plus fast, reliable multi-round execution. It pairs a 196B-parameter foundation with only 11B active parameters, interleaved 3:1 sliding-window/full attention, and Multi-Token Prediction (MTP-3) to cut the latency and cost of long agentic interactions. RL post-training combines verifiable rewards with preference feedback while staying stable under large-scale off-policy training, letting the model keep improving on math, code, and tool use.

## Architecture

- **Sparse MoE**: 196B total parameters, 11B active per token — most of the frontier-model capacity at a fraction of the inference cost.
- **Interleaved 3:1 sliding-window / full attention**: most attention layers use a bounded window ([[Sliding window attention]]); every fourth layer keeps full attention so long-range information can still propagate, without paying full O(n²) cost everywhere.
- **[[Multi-Token Prediction]] (MTP-3)**: predicts multiple tokens per step, reducing the per-turn latency that compounds badly across multi-round agentic interactions.

## Training

A scalable RL framework combines **verifiable signals** (checkable correctness, à la [[RLVR]]) with **preference feedback**, and is engineered to remain stable under **large-scale off-policy training** — letting the model train on rollouts generated by earlier policy versions without the usual instability that introduces. This enables consistent self-improvement across mathematics, code, and tool-use domains.

## Key Benchmark Results

| Benchmark | Score |
|---|---|
| IMO-AnswerBench | 85.4% |
| LiveCodeBench-v6 (2024.08–2025.05) | 86.4% |
| tau2-Bench | 88.2% |
| BrowseComp (with context management) | 69.0% |
| Terminal-Bench 2.0 | 51.0% |

Comparable to frontier-class closed models such as GPT-5.2 xHigh and Gemini 3.0 Pro, despite running with only 11B active parameters.

## Why It Matters

Step 3.5 Flash is a data point for how far the efficiency frontier has moved: frontier-comparable agentic performance no longer requires activating hundreds of billions of parameters per token. The combination of sparse MoE, sliding-window attention, and multi-token prediction specifically targets what makes agents slow — long multi-round interactions — rather than optimizing single-turn benchmark scores in isolation.

## Limitations

- Benchmarked mainly on agent/coding/math tasks; broader general-capability comparisons (e.g. open-ended writing, multilingual) aren't part of the reported results.
- "Comparable to" frontier closed models on these benchmarks doesn't necessarily mean parity across every task or in production deployment conditions.
